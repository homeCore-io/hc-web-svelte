# 13 · The interface as a whole, and looks anyone can make

**Status:** agreed, 2026-11-02 (every decision in §19 is made). Building
follows the steps in §18, each mocked up first where people see it.

**What it builds on.** D1 (generated, with changes on top), D2 and C1 (cards),
D7 (displays), D9 (tokens; outside cards as code), 02 (the parts of the app),
03 (cards), 06 (visual design), 11 (one page document, one renderer, the card
kind registry), 12 (Lit, which proved the idea). Where this changes one of
them, it says so (§17).

**How to read it.** §1 to 3 say what the interface is and the one model behind
it. §4 to 8 are the layers of that model, §9 is arranging (by hand and by
dragging) in every look, and §10 is displays. §11 and 12 are how people and
code make new looks. §13 places every idea from the Home Assistant research.
§14 walks the whole app screen by screen, so nothing is left outside the
model. §15 to 19 are storage, what happens to what's built, changes to
earlier decisions, the steps, and the decisions.

## 1. Why one design for the whole interface

Lit was built as "a second way of drawing rooms". It works, and it showed
three things that a second drawing can't do:

1. **Arranging.** A Lit room is its own drawing, so Customize and Edit fall
   back to Classic. Arranging has to work in every look, in place.
2. **Making looks.** People who don't write code must be able to make a look
   in the app: start from one, change what they like, name it.
3. **Extending looks in code.** A plugin or a developer must be able to add a
   new way of drawing something, or a whole look, without the app changing.

And the twenty most used Home Assistant dashboard add-ons each fix one gap in
Home Assistant's own dashboards. Taken one at a time they would become twenty
features with twenty ideas of what a card is, which is the problem those
add-ons exist to work around (01 §3). They have to land in one model.

So this design starts from the interface as a whole, and treats Classic and
Lit as two looks written in it.

## 2. Principles

Kept from 01, 03 and D9, and added to:

1. **Generated first, then changed** (D1). Every page starts from the house.
   A look changes how the house is presented; arranging changes the page.
2. **A card says what something is doing at a glance and offers at most one
   obvious action** (03 §1). Every drawing keeps this.
3. **Derived from what a device declares**, never from its brand (03 §1). A
   new plugin's devices get sensible drawings on day one.
4. **One design language.** The house looks like one thing whichever look is
   chosen. A look chooses among drawings that share tokens, states and rules;
   it never brings its own idea of a card.
5. **Arranging works everywhere.** Customize, Edit, Add, Move to, sections of
   your own and card settings work the same in every look, because they work
   on the page document, and a look only shapes how the document starts and
   how it's drawn.
6. **Nothing is hidden by a look.** A drawing that can't draw a device falls
   back to Classic's. A grouping that has no place for something puts it in
   "Also here". Alerts are always first (11 §2.6).
7. **Settings, not code, for everything a person can choose.** No YAML, no
   CSS, no templates. What Home Assistant users do with card-mod and
   button-card is a choice in the look editor here.
8. **Code extends through one contract** (D9b): custom elements, the `host`
   interface, the design tokens. The same contract plugins already use for
   their screens and cards.

## 3. The model: a look is a set of choices in five layers

```
            ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  Look      │ Theme · Feel · Drawings · Frame · Grouping              │  ← chosen as a whole (S1)
            └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                 │        │        │          │         │
   app-wide ─────┴────────┘        │          │         │
   household pages ────────────────┴──────────┴─────────┘
                                                        │
  Page document (11 §2.1)  ◄── generated by Grouping ───┘
       ▲
       │  household changes, then the person's (M2 to M4): arranging
```

| Layer        | What it decides                                                                                           | Where it applies                                                               | Today                                                                         |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Theme**    | colors, light or dark                                                                                     | everywhere (as today)                                                          | D9a, 06 (Amber, Sage, Harbour, Plum)                                          |
| **Feel**     | spacing, density, corners, type sizes, motion                                                             | household pages and displays; a look someone makes may reach every screen (S9) | fixed values in 06                                                            |
| **Drawings** | how each kind of thing is drawn, at each size                                                             | every page that draws cards; the details popup's header                        | one drawing per kind; Lit's for lights, switches, scenes                      |
| **Frame**    | the pieces around the cards: a room's header, what needs someone, status chips, "More", a display at rest | household pages and displays                                                   | Lit's header, problems, rest view                                             |
| **Grouping** | how a generated page is split into sections, and which drawing each section starts with                   | generated pages: Home, rooms                                                   | room sections fixed (Controls, Scenes, Sensors, Other); Home's sections fixed |

A **look** names one choice in each layer. Classic and Lit are two looks
written this way. The built-in looks stop at household pages and displays;
settings and the other admin screens keep one look of their own, with only
the theme. A look someone makes may choose to reach every screen (S9, §11).

### 3.1 How a choice is found

For any single choice (say, how the Office desk lamp is drawn), the first
that says wins:

1. this card's own setting on this page (the person's layer, then the house's)
2. the person's look
3. the house's look (or a display's own, D7)
4. the look's defaults
5. Classic, the built-in fallback

This is 11 §2.4 extended by one step (the look), so every existing setting
keeps working.

### 3.2 Names

"Style" already means Home's section layouts (11 §2.3, the room field, room
tiles, rooms in the sidebar). To avoid two meanings:

- **Look**: the whole set of choices (Classic, Lit, the house's own).
- **Home layout**: what 11 called a style. It becomes part of a look's
  grouping for Home (§8.3), and stays choosable in Customize as today.

## 4. Theme

Unchanged from D9a and 06: color tokens only, light and dark, a house theme,
a person's own, a display's own.

The **color language** (S7, decided): things show their own color.

- **A light that reports its color** shows that color, in its own card and in
  a room's glow.
- **A scene's swatch** shows the colors the scene sets.
- **Amber** means _on_ for anything with no color of its own: a switch, a
  TV, a mode.
- **Red** only means _needs someone_.

This replaces 06 §1's "amber means on, and nothing else is colored" (§17).

## 5. Feel

Today's spacing, corners, type sizes and motion become tokens a look sets.
Every component reads them. On the screens a look reaches (S9) they follow
the look; elsewhere they keep Classic's values.

| Token group | Values                     | Classic           | Lit          |
| ----------- | -------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------ |
| Density     | roomy · standard · compact | standard          | compact      |
| Corners     | square · soft · round      | soft (14px cards) | round (18px) |
| Type        | scale 0.9 to 1.15          | 1.0               | 1.0          |
| Numbers     | Plex Mono or the body face | Plex Mono         | Plex Mono    |
| Motion      | still · gentle · lively    | still             | lively       |
| Depth       | flat · borders · shadows   | 06's rule         | borders      |

**Motion** is welcome (S8, decided): _still_ means nothing moves unless
something changed (06 as written, and Classic's); _gentle_ allows slow
ambient motion, a display breathing at rest, a scene's colors sweeping
once when it runs; _lively_ adds looping animations (Rive, §12.4) and
transitions as cards change, and is Lit's. Reduced motion always wins over
the look.

## 6. Drawings

### 6.1 What a drawing is

A **drawing** draws one kind of thing at one or more sizes. It extends 11's
card kind registry: today a kind has one component; now a kind has several
drawings, and a look picks one per kind.

```
drawing
  id          lit.light.dimcard | classic.device | lit.door.picture | …
  draws       kinds it draws (light, dimmer, group) or traits (dims, opens)
  forms       compact · standard · large, and which it prefers
  settings    a descriptor (form engine) adding to the card's sheet
              (a door's "Opened by", a light's "Show its own color")
  states      it must draw offline, pending, alarming (03 §5, C8, C9)
  fallback    always Classic's drawing for the same kind
  source      built in · a plugin's (§12)
```

A drawing receives exactly what a card gets today: the device as homeCore
describes it (`cardFor`), its settings on this page, its size, the person's
units, and the `host` for commands. It never reaches into the app.

### 6.2 What every drawing gets for free

- **Arranging.** In Customize and Edit every drawing is wrapped by the same
  tools (Arrange): hide, star, move, Move to, settings, size, add-in-place
  slots. A drawing writes no arranging code.
- **The card's sheet.** Its settings join the kind's sheet (symbol, size,
  main reading) through the form engine; no hand-written sheet.
- **Pending and failure.** A command not yet confirmed, a device offline, a
  command refused: drawn from one shared rule (C8), the drawing only says
  where.
- **Fallback.** If a drawing throws, stalls or can't draw a device, Classic's
  drawing takes its place (D9b's containment rule, for built-in drawings too).

### 6.4 Drawings made in homeCore (D1, D2)

Besides choosing a drawing, a person can make one in the look editor
("Make a drawing…" on the Drawings tab). It is the idea of Home Assistant's
Mushroom template cards and button-card, built in. There are three levels,
and a drawing can use any mix of them:

1. **Parts**, for anyone. A drawing is put together from parts:
   - **shape:** card, tile, disc, line, or a panel tile;
   - **fill:** none, the thing's level (as Lit's lights), its own color, or a
     picture (a door, a symbol);
   - **shows:** name, state, a reading, an icon, a range bar, a day graph;
   - **tap:** switch it, open it, run it, or nothing;
   - **colors** for on, off, and needs someone.

   It starts from an existing drawing ("Start from Lit's rocker"), so there
   is no blank one.
2. **Expressions**, for anyone. Any part can be set by an expression instead
   of a fixed choice: a label that reads `level > 50 ? "Bright" : "Dim"`, a
   color that follows the temperature, an icon that changes with a door. The
   language is small and safe, made for this, and is not JavaScript: it
   reads the thing (its state, readings, name, room, how long ago it
   changed), compares, does arithmetic, picks between values and formats
   them. It cannot reach anything else, and an expression that fails shows
   the part's plain value.
3. **Code**, for admins. The drawing's Code tab takes HTML and CSS with holes
   for the thing's values, and JavaScript that receives the thing and the
   host (to send commands) and returns what to draw, as button-card's
   templates and custom fields do. It runs as a custom element in its own
   shadow root, the same contract as a plugin's drawing (§12.1), so it is
   contained the same way (§12.5).

**Who.** Parts and expressions are data, so anyone who may make a look may
make them, and they travel in look files that anyone may import. Code runs
in the browser of everyone who sees the look, signed in as them, so only an
admin may write it, save it or import a look that has it (S4); the look
editor marks such a drawing "Has code" and shows who last changed it.
CSS on its own is allowed for anyone with `url()` and `@import` removed, so
it can't call out of the house.

**Editing.** The drawing editor is a split view like the look editor: its
parts, expressions and code on one side; on the other, the drawing on three
of the house's own devices of that kind, at each size, and in its offline,
pending and needs-someone states. Errors show under the field that caused
them, with the line for code.

**Stored** in the look, under `drawings` (§15), as
`{ id, name, draws, from, parts, expressions, code? }`. A look file carries
them; an exported look with code says so before it's saved.

**As built in step 4:**

- "Admin" for code is homeCore's `plugins:write` scope, the one for
  installing plugins. The UI server checks it: a document that adds or
  changes code is refused for anyone else, who may still keep an admin's
  code unchanged, or remove it.
- Code is the body of `draw(thing)` and returns HTML. It runs in a web
  worker and is stopped after 300 ms, so code that loops or throws can't
  stall a screen; that card is drawn as Classic draws it. A tap does what the
  drawing's parts say (switch, open, nothing), so code never sends commands
  itself.
- Drawings can be made for lights, switches, doors and locks, readings and
  Watching. Scenes have their two built-in drawings only, for now.
- The expression language is `looks/expr.ts`: the thing's values (name, on,
  level, color, room, state, temperature, humidity, available, alarm, kind),
  arithmetic, comparisons, `? :`, and round, floor, ceil, abs, min, max,
  upper, lower, fixed.
- When a look doesn't draw lights the Lit way, lights that share a name
  ("Holiday Lights 1" to "4") are separate cards, as Classic has them.
- The editor's preview shows a page as the look starts it, before anyone
  arranges it. A room's sections and Home's are reordered with arrows;
  dragging them in the editor is still to come.

## 8. Grouping

### 8.1 What it is

A grouping is how a **generated** page (Home, a room) is made into the page
document. It is a list of **rules**; each rule makes one section:

```
rule
  id          lights | switches.power | doors | watching | more | …  (stable: §8.4)
  title       "Lights"
  takes       kinds and traits: kind=light; dims; powers something;
              opens (doors, gates); needs someone; rarely used; reads
              temperature; a symbol; in this room
  layout      grid · row · list · field · tiles (11 §2.3)
  drawing     the drawing its cards start with (a person can still change one)
  order       where it comes
```

Things are taken by the first rule that takes them; what no rule takes goes
to "Also here", so a grouping can't lose a device (principle 6).

**Traits** are what homeCore already knows about a device, in words a person
understands. They are the same words the look editor shows (§11): "lights
that dim", "anything that powers something", "doors and gates", "rarely
used", "needs someone now". This is auto-entities built in, without a query
language.

### 8.2 Rooms

Classic's room grouping is today's four sections. Lit's is doors, lights,
switches (lights; power and water), scenes, readings, Watching, More. Both
become rule lists. Because the result is the page document, **a Lit room is
arranged in place**, with Lit's drawings, and the LitRoom component goes away
(§16).

### 8.3 Home

Home keeps its sections (alerts, chips, big numbers, pinned, favorites,
scenes and modes, rooms, playing, worth knowing: G-a to G-e). A look's Home
grouping sets their order, which start hidden, and their layouts. **Home
layouts** (room field, tiles, sidebar) stay what they are, presets a person
can pick in Customize, and a look can name one as its default.

### 8.4 Each look keeps its own arrangements

Looks can be very different (S3, decided), so each look keeps its own
arrangement of every page: its moves, hides, renames, sections of your own,
sizes and card settings. Arranging the Living Room in Lit changes nothing in
Classic, and switching back finds Classic as it was left.

- **A new look starts with a copy** of the arrangements of the look it was
  made from ("Start from Lit" brings Lit's arrangements with it).
- **The same for the house and each person:** the house's arrangement of Lit,
  then a person's own on top of it (M2 to M4), and the same for every other
  look.
- **Kept whatever the look:** what belongs to the person or the house rather
  than to a way of drawing: starred favorites, the phone bar, pages people
  made (their content is kept per look like any page), names given to rooms
  and devices in homeCore.
- **Today's arrangements** become Classic's, so nothing anyone has arranged
  is lost, and Lit starts from its own grouping.

### 8.5 Pages people make

Hand-built pages (G27) have no grouping: they are what their makers built.
Drawings, frame pieces they added, feel and theme apply.

## 9. Arranging, in every look

Arranging is Customize (a person's own changes) and Edit (the house's), as
today (N6). It works on the page document, never on a look's drawing, so it
is the same in every look: each drawing is wrapped by the same arranging
tools (§6.2).

### 9.1 What can be arranged

- **Cards:** move within a section or into another, hide and show, remove
  what was added, change size, rename, open the card's settings, star.
- **Sections:** move, hide and show, rename, change layout, and in the room
  field move between columns (G32).
- **Sections of your own:** add, rename, delete (G17). Generated sections
  can't be deleted (the house would only make them again), but can be hidden,
  which takes them off the page until shown again (S6).
- **Frame pieces** are cards (§7), so a room's header, its status chips and
  More are arranged like anything else.
- **Adding** in place, with the + between cards (G-f), and from the Add sheet.

### 9.2 Dragging

Dragging is a second way to do what the buttons do. It writes the same
changes (`move` with `before` and `into`, 11 §2.5), so undo, the household
and personal layers, saving behind and conflicts work as they do now.

- **Picking up.** With a mouse, press on a card and move. On a touch screen,
  press and hold (about 400 ms), then move. Outside arranging, press and hold
  keeps its job as the star and hide shortcut (N6); while arranging it picks
  the card up. A section is picked up by its heading.
- **While dragging.** The card lifts (a shadow, slightly larger) and the
  others make room: a "Goes here" gap opens where it will land, the same mark
  the + slots use (G-f). A section that can't take the card is dimmed, and
  says why on hover ("Favorites holds only starred things"). Near the top or
  bottom of the screen the page scrolls. On a phone the bar hides while a
  card is held.
- **Dropping.** It lands in the gap, is marked "Added" or "Moved" for a
  moment, and the change is saved. Escape, or letting go away from any gap,
  puts it back.
- **From the Add sheet** (wide screens): a thing in the list can be dragged
  onto the page, landing where it's dropped, as an alternative to a + slot.
- **In the look editor** (§11): a grouping's sections, and Home's sections,
  reorder by dragging too.

### 9.3 Never only by dragging

Every move can still be made without dragging: up and down, Move to, the +
slots. Keyboards can pick a card up with Space, move it with the arrow keys
and drop it with Space again, and a screen reader hears where it is ("Attic
Light, after Holiday Lights 1, in Pinned").

### 9.4 Rules dragging keeps

- Alerts stay first (11 §2.6); nothing can be dropped above them.
- A section that holds only certain things keeps to them: Favorites only
  what's starred, the room field only rooms.
- A person's layer can hide and move, never widen what they can see (D8).
- A card dragged into another section is an arrangement, kept across looks
  by the section's stable id (§8.4).

### 9.5 Resizing by dragging

Decided (S12) on one condition: it has to be clean. So it snaps, never free:

- While arranging, a card that has more than one size shows a small handle
  on its lower right corner.
- Dragging it outlines the next size the card can take (compact, standard,
  large, and large spanning two columns where the section's layout allows),
  and letting go there picks it. It writes the same `form` change the size
  buttons write.
- The card's sheet keeps its size buttons, so resizing never needs dragging.
- A drawing says which sizes it draws (§6.1); a card can't be dragged to a
  size its drawing doesn't have.

## 10. Displays

A display (D7) has its own look, as it has its own theme, defaulting to the
house's. Its rest view, its timeout and its motion are the look's frame and
feel, plus a display's own settings (night, awake). Unlocked with a PIN, it
shows the person's look while unlocked.

## 11. Making a look in the app

For people who never write code.

**Where.** Settings › Look, a **Looks** list: Classic, Lit, and the house's
own, with the house's default marked. Me has the person's choice (L1).

**Start.** "New look" copies one ("Start from Lit"). There is no blank look;
every look is complete from the first moment.

**Edit, with a live preview.** The editor is a split view: choices on one
side, a live preview on the other, of a room the person picks (default: the
busiest), drawn with the house's own devices. On a phone the preview is
above and the choices scroll under it.

The choices, in plain words, each as pictures where it's a drawing:

1. **Colors**: a theme, and "Lights in their own colors" on or off.
2. **Feel**: four sliders (roomy to compact, square to round, small to large
   type, still to lively), each showing the preview change as it moves.
3. **How things are drawn**: for each kind of thing in the house (only the
   kinds the house has), the drawings side by side, drawn with one of the
   house's own devices of that kind. Tap to choose.
4. **A room's frame**: header plain or glowing; problems first; status chips;
   More.
5. **How a room is grouped**: the sections in order, each a row with its
   name, what it takes ("lights that dim"), its layout and its drawing.
   Drag to reorder, tap to rename or change, add, remove. "Back to Lit's".
6. **Home**: its sections' order and layouts, and a default Home layout.
7. **Displays**: rest view on or off, after how long.
8. **Where it reaches**: household pages and displays (as the built-in
   looks), or every screen, settings included (S9).

**Save, share, import.** A look is saved to the house (by anyone who may
edit the house's pages) or to the person. **Export** writes it to a file,
with any of the house's own pictures it uses; **Import** adds a look from a
file, whether it was exported from another homeCore or made offline in code
(§12.3). A look made from Lit says so, and "What changed from Lit" lists the
differences, so a person can always find their way back.

**Who.** As pages (G28): house looks by people who edit the house's pages;
anyone may make their own.

## 12. Extending looks in code

For plugin authors and developers. One contract, the one plugins already use
(D9b): a standard custom element, handed the `host`, inheriting the tokens.

### 12.1 A drawing from a plugin

A new slot, `look.drawing`, in a plugin's `plugin.toml` UI declaration, next
to `device.card` and `dashboard.card`:

```
[[ui.slots]]
slot     = "look.drawing"
element  = "acme-light-orb"
draws    = ["light", "dimmer"]      # kinds, or traits
forms    = ["standard", "large"]
label    = "Orb"
settings = "acme-light-orb-settings" # optional: adds to the card's sheet
```

It appears among the drawings in the look editor (§11.3) for those kinds,
labelled with its plugin. It gets §6.2 for free. `device.card` (a plugin's own
card for its own devices) stays as it is and is shown as that device's
drawing.

### 12.2 A frame piece from a plugin

The same with `look.piece`: a header, a rest view, a status strip.

### 12.3 A look as a bundle, made in homeCore or offline in code

A look is data (§15), so it travels as a **look bundle**: one file holding
the look, and any drawings and pictures it needs.

- **Made in homeCore:** the look editor (§11) exports a bundle.
- **Made offline in code:** a developer writes a look bundle the way a plugin
  is written today, from a template, with drawings as custom elements and the
  look as a file, and previews it against a sandbox homeCore before building
  it into a bundle. An SDK command builds and checks it (the look's shape,
  that every drawing it names is in it or built in).
- **Imported:** Settings › Look › Import takes either kind of bundle. A
  bundle with drawings in code is installed by an admin, like a plugin (S4);
  one with only choices can be imported by anyone, into their own looks.
- **From a plugin:** a plugin can also carry looks and drawings in its own
  release, installed with it.

### 12.4 Animated drawings (Rive)

The host lends one Rive runtime to any drawing that asks, loaded only then
(Home's size budget, D12), and binds a device's readings to the animation's
state-machine inputs by name (`heating`, `brightness`, `open`). A drawing
ships its `.riv` file in its plugin's `ui/` folder. Motion _still_ shows the
first frame; _gentle_ plays transitions; _lively_ plays loops. This is where
the old widget plan's Rive section (widget_descriptor_protocol §10.3) lands,
and it gets written into the plugin docs, which it isn't today.

### 12.5 Containment

As D9b: a drawing that throws or stalls is replaced by Classic's for that
card, and the look editor marks it. A drawing has the same performance budget
as a card (D12), measured in development and refused in the editor if it
fails badly.

## 15. Storage

All presentation, so on the UI server (M1), in the documents that exist:

```
HouseDoc
  looks:   [ look ]          the house's own looks
  look:    "lit" | id        the house's default (12 already stores "lit"/"classic")
  pages:   { lookId: { pageKey: [ change ] } }    each look's arrangements (§8.4)
PersonDoc
  looks:   [ look ]          their own
  look:    id                their choice (12 already stores it)
  pages:   { lookId: { pageKey: [ change ] } }    their own, per look
  favorites, bar, built      kept whatever the look
DisplayDoc
  look:    id                a display's own (D7)

look
  id, name, from            "from": the look it started as (for "What changed")
  theme                     palette id
  feel                      { density, corners, type, motion, depth }
  drawings                  { kind: drawing id }
  frame                     { header, needs, chips, more, rest: { on, after } }
  grouping
    room: [ rule ]
    home: { order, hidden, layouts, homeLayout }
  reaches                   "household" | "everywhere" (S9)
  version
```

Built-in looks (Classic, Lit) and plugins' looks are not stored; they are
read from the app and the plugins. A look names drawings by id; a drawing
that's gone falls back to Classic's (principle 6). Migration: 12's
`look: "lit"` reads as the built-in Lit, and today's `pages` become
Classic's (§8.4). A bundle (§12.3) is a look plus its drawings and pictures.

**As built in step 3** (`src/lib/looks/model.ts`):

- Arrangements stay in the one `pages` map, under `look|page` keys (Classic's
  under the bare page key, as before), instead of a map per look. Nothing had
  to move, and a look with none of its own falls back through `from`.
- A look's drawings are chosen per family (lights, switches, scenes, doors,
  readings, watching), each `classic` or `lit` for now. Plugin drawings
  (step 5) add ids to these lists.
- The frame has only `rest` so far; `depth` in feel and `grouping.home`
  wait for the look editor (step 4), which needs them.
- A look file is `{ "homeCoreLook": 1, "look": { … } }`, choices only. Anyone
  may add one to their own looks; the house's looks are changed by people
  who may edit the house. A look's id that's taken is renamed on the way in.
- Reading a look (`readLook`) fills what it leaves out from the look it was
  made from, else Classic, clamps what's out of range, and drops what it
  can't use, so a hand-written or older file never breaks a page.

## 18. Steps

Mocked up first wherever people see something new; checked on the sandbox
before the next step.

1. **Foundation.** Feel tokens in every component; grouping rules for rooms
   (Classic's and Lit's) producing the page document; drawings in the
   registry; frame pieces; LitRoom removed. Customize and Edit work in Lit.
   Nothing new to choose yet.
2. **Dragging** (§9.2 to 9.4): cards and sections, from the Add sheet, by
   keyboard, in every look. Mocked up first.
3. **The look model.** Looks as data in the documents, built-ins read from
   the app, a person's and a display's choice, export and import.
4. **The look editor** in Settings › Look (§11). Mocked up first.
5. **Code.** `look.drawing`, `look.piece` and `look.symbol` slots in the SDK
   and `host`; plugin docs; one plugin drawing as the proof.
6. **Fold-ins**, in three batches, each mocked up: (a) compact chips, row
   drawings, min/max band, batteries; (b) room sheet, artwork media, joined
   section layout; (c) chart drawing, remote pad, clock and weather.
7. **Rive**: the runtime lent by the host, motion levels, an animated
   thermostat as the proof, docs.
8. **Later**: floor-plan pages, energy flow, and the kinds homeCore doesn't
   have yet.

## 19. Decisions

### Decided (the user, 2026-11-02)

- **S1. A look is chosen as a whole.** Mixing is done by making a look (§11),
  so "Lit's grouping with Classic's cards" is a look of its own.
- **S2. No per-room looks.** A card's own drawing choice (§3.1, step 1)
  covers a room that needs something drawn differently.
- **S3. Each look keeps its own arrangements** (§8.4), because looks can be
  very different.
- **S5. Looks are made in homeCore or offline in code**, travel as bundles,
  and are imported and exported to share (§11, §12.3).
- **S6. Home's grouping** orders, hides and lays out Home's sections and
  names a default Home layout, and sections can still be added, removed and
  deleted (§9.1: your own deleted, generated ones hidden).
- **S7. Things show their own color** (§4).
- **S8. Motion is welcome.** Three levels (§5); Classic still, Lit lively;
  displays follow their look; reduced motion always wins.
- **S9. The built-in looks stop at household pages and displays.** Settings
  and the admin screens keep their own look, with only the theme. A look
  someone makes may choose to reach every screen (§11, "Where it reaches").
- **S12. Resizing by dragging,** snapping between a card's sizes (§9.5).
- **Home layouts** is the name for what 11 called styles (§3.2).
- **Drag and drop** is part of arranging, in every look (§9).
- **American spelling** in the app and its documents: color, favorite,
  customize (§17).

- **S4. Drawings that come in code are installed by an admin**, as any
  plugin is; once installed, everyone can choose them. A bundle with only
  choices (no code) can be imported by anyone into their own looks.
- **S11. The device sheet's top draws the device in your look** (in Lit, a
  light's drag-to-dim card); the rest of the sheet stays as it is today, the
  same in every look.
- **S10. The order of the Home Assistant ideas** is §13.1's, after the
  foundation, dragging and the look editor.

### Decided on the step 4 mockup (the user, 2026-11-02)

- **E1. One editor for every look of one's own.** Me opens the same editor
  as Settings › Look, with every tab, for a person's own looks.
- **E2. The Rooms tab groups every room;** arranging one room stays on that
  room's page (§8.4).
- **E3. The tabs, in order:** Drawings, Feel, Colors, Frame, Rooms, Home,
  Displays, Reaches.
- **D1. Drawings can be made in the editor** from parts and expressions, by
  anyone who may make a look (§6.4).
- **D2. Code can be added in the editor,** as button-card allows: HTML, CSS
  and JavaScript in a drawing's Code tab, by admins only (§6.4, S4).
